Thinkers Timeline

Roughly a hundred thinkers and a handful of shared concepts from Grounding the Unconscious, Genealogies of Desire, and Constellations of History, laid out as one vertical chronological timeline. Top is earlier; bottom is later. Click any box to jump to where it's actually argued out — this page is an index, not a fourth essay.

NOTE — years are layout anchors (floruit or a key work), not birth–death biographies and not a claim of precision. Boxes marked c. are especially approximate: ancient Near Eastern / Axial dates, multi-author clusters, and composition-vs-publication cases. Known caveats: Egyptian–Mesopotamian dream craft spans many centuries (the ~1200 BCE pin is New Kingdom / early omen literature, not a single book); Zoroaster's floruit is among the most contested in the set (pin ~1000 BCE is conventional, not consensus); early Upanishads and the Buddha's dates are contested by decades or more; Laozi's authorship and date are themselves disputed; Confucius is traditional 551–479 BCE; Socrates is pinned to 399 BCE (death), Plato·Aristotle to ~375 BCE (Republic-era akrasia, not Aristotle's later Ethics); Leibniz's New Essays were written c. 1704 but published 1765; Schopenhauer's World as Will is conventionally 1819 though issued late 1818; Freud's Interpretation of Dreams is conventionally 1900 though it appeared late 1899; Lacan's pin is the 1953 Rome discourse / RSI turn (Mirror Stage canonical text is 1949); Baudrillard·Lyotard is clustered at 1974 (Mirror of Production 1973, Libidinal Economy 1974); Fechner is pinned to Elemente der Psychophysik (1860), not a later reprint. Period washes are the same kind of approximation. Edges are illustrative — for the precise, collision-checked version of any single lineage, follow the click-through to its source page. The timeline keeps the same "who gets a box" judgment calls the three source diagrams already made, groups tightly-coupled co-authors the way they do (Deleuze & Guattari, Adorno & Horkheimer), and quietly drops pure biographical asides.

Three different projects, one recurring shape: something in a person or a society that isn't fully available to inspection, and a century-plus argument about how much of it can be mapped, grounded, or organized before the mapping itself runs out of road. Grounding the Unconscious asks whether any of it is in the brain. Genealogies of Desire and Constellations of History ask whose interests it serves and whether it can be built differently. This page doesn't answer either question — it's just the index of everyone who's tried.
