Thinkers Timeline

Thinkers and shared concepts from Grounding the Unconscious, Genealogies of Desire, Constellations of History, and Technics and Time, laid out as one vertical chronological timeline — plus the companion consciousness map figures (Turing through Orch OR / IIT, plus Seth, Barrett, Varela, Kastrup, and Albahari) so that page’s cast isn’t orphaned here. Top is earlier; bottom is later. Click any box to jump to where it's actually argued out — this page is an index, not a fifth essay. Prefer a cohort over a chronology? There is an optional browse-by-current view. For novelists shaped by these thinkers, see Fiction of the maps.

NOTE — years are layout anchors: when someone was active, or the date of a key work — not birth–death biographies and not a claim of precision. Boxes marked c. are especially approximate: ancient Near Eastern / Axial dates, multi-author clusters, and composition-vs-publication cases. Known caveats: Egyptian–Mesopotamian dream craft spans many centuries (the ~1200 BCE pin is New Kingdom / early omen literature, not a single book); when Zoroaster was active is among the most contested in the set (pin ~1000 BCE is conventional, not consensus); early Upanishads and the Buddha's dates are contested by decades or more; Laozi's authorship and date are themselves disputed; Confucius is traditional 551–479 BCE; Socrates is pinned to 399 BCE (death), Plato·Aristotle to ~375 BCE (Republic-era akrasia, not Aristotle's later Ethics); Aristotle·techne is a lexical pin (~350 BCE), not a separate Ethics monograph date; Leibniz's New Essays were written c. 1704 but published 1765; Schopenhauer's World as Will is conventionally 1819 though issued late 1818; Freud's Interpretation of Dreams is conventionally 1900 though it appeared late 1899; Jung is pinned to the 1912 break / Psychology of the Unconscious (collective unconscious), with Sabina Spielrein's 1912 destruction paper as the hinge often erased from the father–son story — his 1930s race/nation texts are argued in the Grounding margin, not as a second timeline pin; Gramsci's hegemony pin is c. 1930 inside the 1929–35 Prison Notebooks; Lacan's pin is the 1953 Rome discourse / RSI turn (Mirror Stage canonical text is 1949; objet a is a separate 1962 concept node from Seminar IX — do not back-project it); Joan Copjec is pinned to 1989 beside Žižek (the orthopsychic-subject essay; Read My Desire is 1994); Alenka Zupančič to Ethics of the Real (2000); Heidegger's Gestell lecture was delivered late 1953 and published 1954; Fanon is pinned to Black Skin, White Masks (1952), not only Wretched of the Earth (1961); Simondon 1958; Rosa's social acceleration to the 2005 German habilitation (English 2013); Jameson's postmodernism book 1991 (essay 1984); Wynter to the 2003 "Unsettling the Coloniality…" essay; Baudrillard·Lyotard clustered at 1974; Popper pinned to Conjectures and Refutations (1963) though falsifiability is already in the 1934/1959 Logic of Scientific Discovery; Fechner to Elemente der Psychophysik (1860). Period washes are the same kind of approximation. Edges are illustrative — for the precise, collision-checked version of any single lineage, follow the click-through to its source page. The timeline keeps the same "who gets a box" judgment calls the source diagrams already made, groups tightly-coupled co-authors the way they do (Deleuze & Guattari, Adorno & Horkheimer; Lorde · hooks), and quietly drops pure biographical asides. Grounding now carries Klein and Segal as the British clinic, plus Copjec and Zupančič as the 1989/Ljubljana half of the Lacan leap rather than as a Genealogies-only annex. Moreton-Robinson is the Australia/white-possessive pin. Klein (1946 schizoid-mechanisms paper; play technique from the 1920s, Envy and Gratitude 1957) is the British object-relations pin on Grounding; Hanna Segal (1957 symbol-formation) is the hinge that meets Lacan's Symbolic without becoming a second linguistics box; Chakrabarty (Provincializing Europe, 2000) and Moreton-Robinson (The White Possessive, 2015) sit inside Constellations' colonial-critique band; Mbembe ("Necropolitics," 2003) lives on Desire beside Foucault; Chun (Programmed Visions, 2011) is the software-studies foil on Technics. Colonial critique lives inside Constellations; techne / Gestell / Stiegler / Hui / Hayles / Chun is its own essay track — Genealogies still keeps Heidegger at Being and Time only. Mead is pinned to Coming of Age in Samoa (1928), with Sex and Temperament (1935) argued in the essay; Benedict to Patterns of Culture (1934); Bateson to Naven (1936) / schismogenesis (the 1972 Steps plateau is the D&G import); Girard to Deceit, Desire and the Novel (1961), with Violence and the Sacred (1972) as the same-year rival to Anti-Oedipus; Taussig to Mimesis and Alterity (1993), via Benjamin's mimetic faculty.

Four different projects, one recurring shape: something in a person or a society that isn't fully available to inspection, and a century-plus argument about how much of it can be mapped, grounded, organized, or technically exteriorized before the mapping itself runs out of road. Grounding the Unconscious asks whether any of it is in the brain. Genealogies of Desire and Constellations of History ask whose interests it serves and whether it can be built differently — including, now, under colonial and racial critique of the culture map itself. Technics and Time asks what tools and digital memory do to the subject who uses them. This page doesn't answer those questions — it's just the index of everyone who's tried.
